Main desk vs lab desk

Two desks run on the same server. The main desk is a meeting on a clock: a head trader plans the whole book a few times a day and holds stocks for days. The lab desk is your rule: an eye on every instrument every minute, a prediction when something changes, and a maximum-loss line and a minimum-profit line on every position.

How each desk reaches a trade

Read each lane left to right. Both desks share the same feeds, the same risk officer and the same execution trader. What differs is who decides, when, and what holds the position afterwards.

One trade on its lines

An example with round numbers, not a real trade. The predictor calls a stock long at 200.00 with a maximum loss of 0.6%, a first profit line of 0.9% and a step of 0.4%. Everything after the fill is arithmetic: no model is asked again.

If it falls firstSold at 198.80The maximum loss line. −0.60%, about −0.70% after costs. For stocks and funds the same stop also rests at the broker, so it holds even if the desk is down.
If it touches 201.80 and turnsSold at 201.80Once the first profit line is reached, coming back to it sells. +0.90% before costs. The stop on the entry is the backstop behind that.
If it keeps risingThe line followsEvery further 0.4% lifts the profit line one step. The position is sold when the price comes back to the latest line, so a run is kept and a reversal is not.
What the lines cannot doPick the directionOn 62 to 74 past sessions the lines alone lost about 0.1% a trade at any width. They keep a loss small; only the prediction can make the average positive.

A day on the lab desk

Times are UTC. Crypto never closes, so the sentinel and the predictor work through the night and the weekend. Stocks and funds join for the New York session and leave before it ends.

Why the head trader's answer was wrong, and why it changed nothing

You asked the lab's chat whether it trades crypto, gold and oil, and it said no. On the lab desk the head trader is not in the path that leads to an order. Find it in the first drawing: it sits outside the loop.

What it was toldThe main desk's briefingIts standing description says "a desk that trades US equities", and its daily briefing listed only the stock universe. I added the lab's agents and never updated what the chat is told. It answered truthfully from wrong information.
Who actually decidesThe predictor, with its own briefingThe predictor has its own prompt and its own material: the instrument's readings, the market, the news, the book's rules and its own track record. It never reads the head trader's briefing, so nothing the chat believed could reach an order.
What is fixedThe chat now knows where it isOn the lab it is told the three markets, who decides, and that it cannot place orders there. It has a tool for the lab's record, open lines and recent calls. Not yet seen live: it needs the update on the server.

What reading the whole branch turned up

The chat's wrong answer was one of a family: places that still assumed the main desk. I read the lab end to end for the rest. Some were only wording. Some would have broken the first stock trade.

Would have gone wrong on a real trade

fixedAn exit could get stuck. The broker's resting stop counted as an order in flight, so the risk officer would have refused the sell that replaces it, after the stop was already cancelled.
fixedA refusal was never heard. The lines engine did not listen for the risk officer saying no. A refused entry blocked that instrument for good; a refused exit was never sent again.
fixedStops the broker rejects. Stock stops were priced to four decimals; the broker takes cents. Three rejections in a row trip the kill switch.
fixedStocks held overnight. The broker's stop is a day order, and a line cannot protect through a gap. Stocks and funds are now closed before the bell, with no new ones in the last 30 minutes.
fixed"Run a cycle" worked on the lab. It would have run the main desk's swing plan on the lab account and opened positions nothing was watching.
fixedOne live stream, two desks. Both accounts share one login, which allows one stream. The lab now polls stock prices every 20 seconds and leaves the stream to the main desk.
fixedSmaller ones: a quiet instrument never reached its time limit; a stale snapshot could close a fresh position; a call too small to buy one share was recorded as acted.

Said the wrong thing

fixedThe chat. Described the main desk's mandate.
fixedThe funnel. Labelled lab instruments "swing candidate" and "intraday candidate" and named the head trader on the fourth check. It now says on lines, called, passed, candidate or watch, with the predictor's reason.
fixedTrade cards. A lab trade showed "no written thesis". It now shows the call that opened it or the line that closed it, with the result after costs.
fixedStatus and controls. "Next cycle" and "open theses" on a desk that has neither; the buttons offered to run a cycle.
