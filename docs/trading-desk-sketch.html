The Autonomous Trading Desk

One head-trader agent runs a research team, an independent risk officer approves or vetoes every order, and an execution agent buys and sells US equities on its own. Two books, swing and intraday. Paper trading first, live trading on measured results. This page is the whiteboard version of the four design documents in the repository.

research and analysis decisions, LLM reasoning risk books and strategies data and features learning and replay storage infrastructure notes

1. The mental model: a trading desk

Think of it as a real desk. The head trader asks the team for research, weighs it, forms a thesis and decides. The risk officer sits beside the head trader, not under it. The journal explains every order after the fact. Nobody calls anybody; every arrow below is an event on a bus.

2. What we decided

Market · decidedUS equitiesNYSE and Nasdaq stocks and ETFs. Deepest data, free paper trading on Alpaca, richest news and fundamentals coverage.
Autonomy · decidedLevel 1: paper, autonomousReal-time decisions with simulated fills and no money at risk. Graduation to live trading on measured results only.
Horizon · decidedBoth booksSwing: positions held days to weeks. Intraday: minutes to hours, flat before the close. Shared data, risk and learning; separate strategies and budgets.
Budget · decidedNo ceiling, free tiers firstEvery data source starts on its free tier and is upgraded only when a defined trigger is hit. Claude Opus 5.5 for every reasoning agent from the start.
Team design · decidedTen LLM roles, one anatomyEach reasoning agent is a role prompt, its own evidence, read-only tools, an output schema, a memory and a scorecard. Analysts work independently; a skeptic argues against every draft thesis; deep dives get a five-call tool budget. Risk, execution and features stay deterministic.

Assumptions applied until you say otherwise

A1 · stackPython 3.12 with asyncio. Standard for trading, machine learning and the Anthropic SDK.
A2 · prediction targetReturn distributions and direction probability, never a single price. Swing at 1, 5 and 20 days; intraday at 15, 30 and 60 minutes.
A3 · universeSwing: about 30 liquid large caps across all 11 sectors. Intraday: 10 to 20 names picked pre-market from the 500 most liquid, plus SPY and QQQ.
A4 · intraday meansMinute-scale decisions. Not sub-second, not market making. LLM latency is seconds.
A5 · short sellingAllowed in both books in paper, with separate caps.
A6 · instrumentsStocks and ETFs only in v1. Options data is a factor, not something we trade.
A7 · paper capitalSet the paper account to the capital you actually intend to deploy, so sizing and day-trader rules are realistic.
A8 · event busRedis Streams from phase 1, in-memory bus for tests. Persistence, replay and consumer groups from day one.
A9 · system of recordPostgreSQL with TimescaleDB for everything operational; Parquet plus DuckDB for research.
A10 · modelsClaude Opus 5.5 for head trader, analysts and post-mortems. Claude Haiku 5.5 for bulk news scoring. LightGBM for the first forecasters.
A11 · deploymentDocker Compose on a local machine while building; a small cloud VM once paper trading runs every day.
A12 · retrainingWeekly walk-forward retraining. A challenger is promoted only if it beats the incumbent out of sample.

3. Two books, one desk

The books differ most in how the LLM takes part. In the swing book the head trader reasons on every decision. In the intraday book it writes a playbook before the open and a deterministic strategy executes bar by bar. No LLM call ever sits between a bar and an order.

4. A day on the desk

All times US Eastern, driven by the exchange calendar so holidays and early closes are handled. The swing cycle for the next session starts right after today's close.

5. Technical architecture

Collectors publish onto one event bus. Every agent subscribes to the topics it needs and publishes what it produces. The stores keep an append-only record of all of it. Backtesting is the same system fed by a replayer under a simulated clock, with a simulated broker swapped in.

The payloads every agent speaks

ResearchReport · every analyst
  • analyst, symbol or market-wide, horizon
  • stance bullish / bearish / neutral, conviction 0 to 1
  • summary in three sentences at most
  • evidence: event ids, one line each
  • risks, and invalidation conditions
  • optional structured numbers
Forecast · each model and the ensemble
  • model id, trained-through date, symbol, horizon
  • expected return, quantiles 5 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 95
  • p(up), p(move beyond threshold)
  • top five feature attributions
  • confidence from the model's own calibration
OrderIntent · head trader or strategy
  • symbol, book, side, target position
  • order type, limit price, time in force
  • thesis id, urgency
  • rationale event ids
RiskDecision · risk officer
  • intent id, decision approved / modified / rejected
  • approved quantity
  • every rule evaluated, with pass or fail and the numbers
  • reason

6. The swing cycle, stage by stage

Each stage has a timeout and a rule for incomplete data. Missing research lowers conviction; missing bars or a failed decision stage fails the cycle, keeps existing positions and sends nothing new.

Events that wake the head trader outside the schedule
  • scored news with impact above threshold on a held or watched name
  • a macro release surprising by more than one standard deviation
  • a thesis invalidation observed by the Swing Strategy
  • a risk limit breach
  • a trading halt on a held name
A re-evaluation is a mini cycle: refresh only the analysts whose domain changed, decide, risk review, execute.
Intraday loop, every minuteBar arrives, Feature Store computes intraday features, Intraday Strategy checks the setups the playbook allows for that name, emits an order intent, risk officer approves inside the book's budget, execution sends. Haiku-scored news updates a per-symbol news state that the strategy reads on the next bar.

7. Thesis, risk officer, autonomy

Thesis · the trader's memoryEvery swing position exists because of a stored thesis, updated as evidence arrives and closed with a post-mortem.
  • symbol, book, direction, status
  • narrative: why we are in this trade
  • evidence: reports and forecasts by event id
  • expected return, horizon, target price
  • invalidation: price-based stop, evidence-based (guidance cut, sentiment flip, sector breakdown), time-based maximum hold
  • size rationale, and every update
Price and time invalidation are checked every bar by the strategy. Evidence invalidation is judged by the head trader. The desk sells when the reason it bought stops being true.
Risk officer · deterministic, versioned, logged
  • Per order: fat-finger bounds, price band, share of 20-day volume, no orders in halts or auctions, order rate limit
  • Per position: max position as a share of equity, max loss per trade, volatility-scaled sizing
  • Per book: gross and net caps, daily loss halt, open position cap, intraday flat by 15:55
  • Portfolio: exposure caps, sector concentration, factor crowding, drawdown ladder (de-risk, then halt), day-trader rule, margin cap
  • Kill switch: manual or automatic on daily loss, drawdown, stale data, broker disconnect, repeated rejects. Closing orders only while engaged.
It never consults an LLM.

8. The learning loop

The desk measures itself. Closed trades are graded, analysts earn or lose credibility, forecasts are checked for calibration, and models are only promoted when they beat what is already running.

9. What moves a stock

Twelve factor families, more than three hundred individual factors in the catalogue. Each has a horizon, an update frequency, a data source and an owning agent. Structured data becomes point-in-time features; unstructured text becomes structured, scored events through the LLM analysts.

v1 build orderReturns, moving averages, RSI, MACD, volatility, relative volume, gaps, opening range, VWAP · valuation, growth, margins, surprises, revisions, short interest, insiders · earnings and dividend calendar, expirations · news relevance, novelty, polarity, impact, call tone · sector relative strength and peers · rates path, CPI, payrolls, ISM, credit, dollar, oil · index trend, VIX, breadth, put-call, seasonality · beta and factor loadings · the full intraday set.
Pitfalls the catalogue is built aroundFirst-reported fundamentals, not restated. Earnings dates as known at the time. Vendor publication timestamps for news, never our fetch time. Delisted names kept in history. Lagged releases such as short interest and 13F aligned to their release date.

10. Stack and vendors

Language and schemasPython 3.12, asyncio, pydantic v2 for every event payload and LLM output. Prompts are versioned files, never inline strings.
Bus and storageRedis Streams. PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB and pgvector. Parquet with DuckDB for research. MLflow for models. Disk, later S3, for documents.
ModelsLightGBM for tabular forecasts, statsmodels and arch for volatility, PyTorch later only if it earns its place.
LLMAnthropic Python SDK. Opus 5.5 with adaptive thinking for decisions, Haiku 5.5 for bulk scoring. Structured outputs, prompt caching, Batch API overnight, stop-reason checks with server-side fallback.
Data feeds · free firstAlpaca Basic for price history, delayed quotes, paper execution and Benzinga news. EDGAR for filings and as-filed fundamentals. FRED for macro. Finnhub and FMP free tiers for calendars and estimate snapshots. FINRA, CFTC, CBOE for positioning. Upgrade triggers: real-time SIP for live intraday paper, estimate revision history, transcripts, options history.
OperationsAPScheduler on the exchange calendar, alpaca-py broker adapter, OpenTelemetry with Prometheus and Grafana, pytest with replay fixtures, Docker Compose.

11. Roadmap

Durations assume one or two people working on it most days. Every phase ends with a review before the next starts.

Proposed gate from paper to live approval
  • at least 3 months and 60 trading days of uninterrupted paper operation
  • swing book positive after costs with drawdown inside the limit; intraday book positive after costs or switched off
  • forecast calibration within tolerance, zero risk-rule bypasses, incidents trending down
  • paper fills reconciled against the broker every day without manual fixes
Phase 1 exit testA scripted signal produces a paper order whose journal entry links intent, risk decision, submission and fill. One historical day replays through the bus with identical agent code. Stale data or a broker disconnect engages the kill switch and alerts.

12. Risks and how the design answers them

RiskDefense built into the design
Lookahead biasTwo timestamps on every event, clock-aware agents, first-reported fundamentals, replay through the same code
OverfittingWalk-forward only, challenger versus incumbent promotion, few parameters, costs always on
LLM overconfidenceStructured outputs with cited evidence, credibility weights, deterministic risk officer above every decision
Weak, decaying signalsEnsemble across independent families, regime awareness, honest after-cost reporting, permission to do nothing
Data gapsPer-collector validation, staleness alarms, fail-safe policies that skip rather than guess
Cost of data and LLM callsHaiku for bulk, Batch API overnight, prompt caching, output cache on replay, daily spend alerts
Broker or feed outageKill switch on disconnect, closing-only mode, reconciliation on reconnect
Regulatory rulesDay-trader and short-sale rules encoded in the risk officer and exercised in paper
